\section*{Overview}

We thank reviewers Vvyc, K6Zq, and GTaK for their constructive feedback and
positive reassessments following the rebuttal. This revision incorporates
the completed rebuttal analyses into the manuscript rather than leaving
them as response-only evidence. The main changes are: (i) a revised
shared-pair ordering metric with symmetric treatment of same-sentence ties;
(ii) recalculated model-level, domain-level, mitigation, and human-validation
results and regenerated data figures; (iii) full per-model absolute fact
inclusion statistics; (iv) an ordering robustness analysis restricted to
responses retaining all six facts; (v) the complete five-dimensional,
two-model grounding-instruction ablation; and (vi) clearer limits on causal
attribution and numerical-specificity interpretation. We preserve the
existing figure designs, generated responses, and human annotation sample.
The previous-round revision letter is retained separately.

Vvyc &
\textbf{Ordering can conflate omission with reordering.} Missing pairs
remain in the original denominator and can increase the oriented score.
&
The Evaluation section replaces the original formula with a direct paired
effect over favorable--adverse pairs whose two facts appear in both
responses. First mentions use all matched sentences; favorable-first is 1,
adverse-first is 0, and a same-sentence tie is 0.5. We average pairs within
each scenario and then scenarios equally. The new appendix ``Ordering
Robustness and Eligible Comparisons'' reports every model, interval, and
eligible count. Effects remain positive for all 12 models and significant
for 11 after the paper's 60-test adjustment; GPT-5.4 Mini is the exception.
\\

Vvyc &
\textbf{Asymmetric grounding instruction.} The neutral prompt alone requires
using only the provided facts and avoiding outside information.
&
The Paired Design and Main Results sections now link to ``Neutral
Grounding-Instruction Ablation.'' We removed only the neutral grounding
sentence, generated 160 new neutral outputs each for Qwen3-8B and
Llama-3.1-8B, and reused the original goal outputs. The appendix reports all
five dimensions with a ten-test BH family. Selection, emphasis, ordering,
and framing remain significant for both models; numerical specificity loss
does not. Revised ordering changes from 0.0922 to 0.0707 for Qwen and
0.0851 to 0.0545 for Llama. We distinguish nonzero post-ablation effects
from tests of before/after differences or equivalence.
\\

Vvyc &
\textbf{Dimension-name styling.} Prefer consistent lowercase and italics in
running text.
&
The evaluation and results narrative uses lowercase italicized dimension
names. Conventional capitalization is retained in headings, tables and
the existing image labels; ``Specificity'' in the images is explicitly
defined as numerical specificity loss in the text and caption.
\\

K6Zq &
\textbf{Near-zero selection does not establish absolute retention.}
Balanced omissions can leave the favorable--adverse imbalance unchanged.
&
The Selection definition and Main Results now explicitly separate imbalance
from absolute inclusion and link to ``Absolute Fact Inclusion and Selection
Interpretation.'' Its table covers all 12 models and both conditions.
Favorable inclusion is 99.31\% versus 98.85\%; adverse inclusion is
99.60\% versus 98.52\%. Complete-six-fact responses are 96.77\% versus
92.86\%. We report the larger adverse-fact decline and model variation,
and remove the inference that a small selection effect proves evidence
retention or absence of selective omission.
\\

K6Zq &
\textbf{Condition ordering on retained facts.} A robustness analysis should
exclude pairs whose order cannot be observed.
&
The shared-pair metric is now the primary definition. The ordering appendix
also reports the stricter all-six-facts subset: 132--154 scenarios per model,
positive effects for all 12 (0.0241--0.1734), and 11 significant after a
separate 12-test adjustment. GPT-5.4 Mini remains uncertain (0.0241;
95\% interval [$-0.0157$, 0.0636]). The manuscript states that these are
conditional estimates, not measurements of omitted evidence.
\\

GTaK &
\textbf{The contrast bundles grounding, role, audience, and objective.}
Changes should not be attributed to the objective sentence alone or
equated automatically with misleading persuasion.
&
The abstract, introduction, Paired Design, conclusions and limitations now
describe institutional role-and-objective conditioning relative to neutral
decision support, not an objective-only causal intervention. The audience
context is supplied in both user messages; the goal system message restates
it. We explain that roles can imply an institutional interest and are
therefore omitted from the neutral baseline. The grounding ablation
addresses that specific asymmetry while preserving this contrast. We
retain the distinction between faithful persuasion and misleading
impression management and avoid claiming that fixed source evidence
guarantees all generated claims are grounded.
\\

GTaK &
\textbf{Ordering credits absent pairs.}
&
The revised shared-pair definition, complete-six-fact robustness table,
and updated main results address the same omission concern raised by Vvyc
and K6Zq. Only one scenario each for DeepSeek v4 Thinking and Llama-3.1-8B
has no shared pair; these models use 159 scenarios and all others use 160.
Undefined effects are excluded, never scored as zero or favorable-first.
\\

GTaK &
\textbf{Specificity measures numerical retention, not all qualifications,
and is not inherently goal-favoring.}
&
The dimension is now called numerical specificity, with the reported
loss score explicitly described as a nondirectional fidelity diagnostic.
The dimension table, paired-effect interpretation, results and limitations
are updated. ``Numerical Specificity and Descriptive Aggregation'' explains
the 132-scenario denominator, numerical-only scope, semantic-attribution
limitation, and appropriate rounding/compression caveat. Losing a number
can downplay a cost or exaggerate a benefit; directional characterization
is assessed separately through framing. The composite is described as
descriptive, not a calibrated misleadingness score.

\section*{Consistency of Results, Figures, and Interpretation}

\textbf{Main results and uncertainty.}
All ordering effects, the descriptive Average, and significance shading
were recomputed. DeepSeek v4 Thinking now has the largest table Average
(0.114), followed by Llama-3.1-8B (0.105). We clarify the pre-existing
aggregation convention: the table averages positive parts of raw dimension
means, whereas category figures use normalized positive-part composites.
The statistical appendix specifies percentile bootstrap intervals from
20,000 scenario resamples, separate 200,000-draw sign-flip tests, and the
60-, 12-, and 10-test correction families for main, complete-subset, and
grounding analyses, respectively.

\textbf{Figures.}
The category radar and neutral/goal dumbbell plot were regenerated using
the original plotting functions, model order, colors, labels, sizes and
scaling rules. The supplied auxiliary radar grid was also regenerated.
Only ordering inputs and quantities derived from ordering were changed.
The condition-profile caption clarifies that ordering coordinates are arm
scores conditioned on shared pairs, not independent absolute output scores.

\textbf{Downstream analyses.}
The mitigation appendix now uses shared-pair ordering: goal/guardrail
effects relative to neutral are 0.0922/$-0.0266$ for Qwen and
0.0851/0.0431 for Llama. Direct guardrail-minus-goal contrasts are computed
independently rather than inferred by subtracting means over different
shared-pair sets. The ambiguity-exclusion rank-correlation range is updated
to 0.952--0.993. The fixed human-validation sample and its annotation counts
are unchanged, but the continuous-score association was recomputed:
Spearman $\rho=0.508$, pair-level permutation $p=0.00018$. We also correct
the sample description to upper/lower-quartile stratified sampling under
the original score implementation, not a newly selected set of extrema.
The two qualitative examples retain their original text; their revised
ordering effects are now stated explicitly.
