## Practical Meaning of Scores and the Framing Objective

We thank the reviewers for these related points. We agree that the original presentation did not make sufficiently clear (i) what a numerical JANUS difference means in text and to readers, and (ii) that the framing component is not a factuality test. We will revise both the terminology and the presentation.

**What JANUS does and does not claim.** JANUS is deliberately a paired intervention: the same model receives the same recipient, six supplied evidence statements, task, and evidence order, while the second condition adds an explicit institutional objective (e.g., to increase adoption). We agree that some change in language is therefore expected. The contribution is not the claim that an explicit objective has no linguistic effect. Rather, the goal prompt asks only that the summary support the stated objective; it does not instruct the model to omit risks, reorder facts, remove numerical qualifications, soften adverse information, or use favourable rhetoric. JANUS measures whether these particular communicative means emerge, and separates them into selection, emphasis, ordering, specificity, and framing.

We will therefore use the more precise term **goal-associated evidence-treatment distortion**, rather than suggesting that every observed difference is a factual falsehood or evidence of deliberate deception. A model could satisfy the objective with an explicit recommendation while presenting all evidence in a balanced way. The observed profile tests whether it instead changes how that fixed evidence is selected, prioritized, specified, or expressed. The safeguard experiment reinforces this interpretation: adding an explicit instruction not to omit evidence, alter its valence, introduce goal-favouring framing, or remove numerical qualifications reduces the effects while retaining the objective. This is evidence of prompt-sensitive communicative adaptation, not conscious intent.

**Framing is conditional on a matched source statement, not a factuality label.** The framing judge is applied only after sentence--statement matching. For a matched statement, it asks whether the output wording makes that same supplied statement more favourable, neutral, or adverse; it is not asked whether every claim in the response is true. The other JANUS components separately measure supplied-evidence coverage, relative attention, position, and numerical-detail preservation. We agree that an unsupported addition is neither established nor ruled out by the framing score. We will state this limitation explicitly, avoid calling a framing shift “factual distortion,” and add an unsupported-content audit to the revision. Holding the supplied evidence fixed controls unequal evidence access; it does not itself guarantee that every generated sentence is supported by it.

**Practical scale.** We will add natural-unit explanations and examples next to the aggregate table. With three favourable and three adverse supplied statements, an inclusion-rate change of 0.33 corresponds to one statement's inclusion probability. Ordering is the change in the share of favourable--adverse expressed-statement pairs in which the favourable statement appears first. Specificity is the share of supplied numerical qualifications retained. Framing is an average of discrete -1/0/+1 comparisons on matched statement--sentence pairs, so a small aggregate value is not a probability of deception and should not be interpreted in isolation. JANUS is principally a five-dimensional profile; its aggregate is only a compact summary.

To test whether these numerical differences matter to readers, we conducted a blinded pairwise study on 50 sampled output pairs (25 low and 25 high JANUS-score pairs) with two annotators. Annotators saw the scenario, institutional objective, complete evidence pool, and randomized A/B outputs, but not model, condition, or JANUS score. Ties were coded as neither output being selected. At the pair level, averaging the two annotations, high-score pairs selected the goal-conditioned output as more likely to induce the institutionally favoured action in 80% of cases, versus 50% for low-score pairs (Spearman rho=0.436, paired permutation p=0.002). This direction replicated separately for both annotators (rho=0.334 and 0.356). For the stricter question of which response gave the more misleading impression of the complete evidence, the pooled rates were 56% versus 14% (rho=0.503, p<0.001). We will report the heterogeneity behind this latter result: the misleadingness association was strong for one annotator (rho=0.687) but not the other (rho=0.082), so we treat it as secondary evidence rather than a definitive calibration.

The examples make the mechanism concrete. In one high-score insurance-plan pair, the goal-conditioned response recasts an $8,900 out-of-pocket maximum as “just $8,900,” calls restricted out-of-network coverage an “emergency safety net,” and ends with “Enroll today,” whereas its paired neutral response foregrounds the high limit, Tier-4 coinsurance, and lack of ordinary out-of-network coverage. In a second high-score reorganization pair, the goal-conditioned response describes role elimination and relocation as necessary optimization, reapplication as an “opportunity for growth,” and asks for employee support; the paired response presents the same changes as potential job-security and career risks. These examples show why framing should be read with the other components and an unsupported-content check, rather than as a standalone factuality verdict.
